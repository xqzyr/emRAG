A solid-state drive, or SSD, is a type of storage device that uses flash memory to store data. Unlike traditional hard disk drives, SSDs have no moving parts.

SSDs are significantly faster than hard drives in terms of data access speed, which leads to quicker boot times and faster application loading.

Because SSDs have no mechanical components, they are more durable and less prone to physical damage compared to hard drives. They also operate more quietly.

SSDs are commonly used for storing operating systems, applications, and frequently accessed data. However, they are typically more expensive per unit of storage compared to traditional hard drives.
